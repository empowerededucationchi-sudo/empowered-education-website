About us

Built by someone who navigated this system — and decided to change it.

Empowered Education was founded on the belief that access to knowledge about how institutions work is as teachable as any academic subject — and far too rarely taught to the students who need it most. This work is driven by research, with a clear goal: legislative and social impact in primary and secondary education.

Our mission

“To advance equity in education by equipping young people with the social capital, professional knowledge, and AI literacy they need to navigate higher education and the workforce — and by helping institutions build the policies, practices, and culture that make that navigation possible. Our work is guided by research and oriented toward lasting legislative and social change in how primary and secondary education systems support students.”

Theodore Barnes did not arrive in higher education with a roadmap. He arrived with a Bachelor of Arts in Criminal Justice — Law and Policy from Benedictine University and the same questions that millions of first-generation and underrepresented students carry: how does this system actually work, who gets to succeed in it, and why does nobody just say that out loud?

His undergraduate focus on law and policy planted the seed of a career-long commitment — not just to understanding how systems operate, but to changing them. He pursued a Master of Science in Education in Higher Education Leadership and Curriculum Development at Purdue University, building the scholarly and practical foundation to work inside institutions with the explicit goal of making them more navigable, more equitable, and more accountable to the students they claim to serve.

“I was one of the students who didn’t know the rules. I figured out the system piece by piece — through trial and error, through mentors who took a chance on me, and through hard lessons. That experience is the curriculum. Everything Empowered Education teaches, I had to learn the hard way first.”

His career moved through enrollment management — building a campus in Rockford, Illinois, then serving as Associate Director of Admissions at the University of Chicago Crown Family School of Social Work. Each role sharpened the same observation: students with real ability arriving at institutional thresholds without the knowledge of how to cross them.

Barnes now serves as an Assistant Professor of Career Development, teaches AI literacy curriculum, and pursues doctoral research at Loyola University Chicago. He is a published scholar and research scientist whose work spans AI literacy, equity in higher education, decarceration, and the historical construction of race — all oriented toward legislative and social impact.

Organizational history

How we got here

2019

Founded

Empowered Education incorporated as a 501(c)(3)

Founded with a focus on career mentoring — connecting high school and college students with career professionals through job shadowing, workshops, and social capital education.

2021

Program development

Bridge Mentoring Program and Capital Workshop developed

The near-peer mentoring model and two-day college student training workshop were designed and refined, establishing the core program architecture still in use today.

2023

Expansion

AI literacy added as a core organizational pillar

As AI tools reshaped education and the workplace, Empowered Education expanded to include AI literacy consulting, policy design, and curriculum development.

2024

Curriculum

AI Career Explorations course developed and deployed

A full 10-module undergraduate AI literacy curriculum — grounded in equity, built for non-technical professionals — developed and became the foundation for consulting and products.

2026

Now

Website relaunch, product launch, and national expansion

Empowered Education relaunches with consulting services, curriculum products, and youth programming — building toward a brick-and-mortar facility and international reach.

Our values

What guides every decision we make

Equity is structural, not decorative

Every program, product, and policy is designed asking: who does this serve, who does this miss, and who does this harm?

Access requires more than open doors

Getting in is not the same as succeeding. We focus on the knowledge — social capital, professional norms, institutional literacy — that makes access real.

Research drives legislative change

Programs matter. Policy matters more. Our work is aimed at systemic change in how education systems are structured and held accountable.

AI must be taught with honesty

AI tools are powerful and useful — and biased, unreliable, and disproportionately harmful to marginalized communities when deployed carelessly. We teach both truths.

Near-peer knowledge is irreplaceable

A person who recently navigated the path carries knowledge no administrator can replicate. Lived experience is a form of expertise.

Revenue funds access

Consulting and products are not the mission — they fund it. Every paid engagement underwrites free programming for students who cannot pay.
